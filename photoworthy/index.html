Photoworthy

A photo mode and photography gameplay in one kit. The camera knows what is in every photo, scores it with reasons players can read, and lets a photo complete quests, fill a journal or make anything happen.

Try the demo in your browser Features Read the manual
  • Unity 6000.0.85f1+
  • Built-in, URP & HDRP
  • No dependencies
  • Full C# source

How it works

A first photo in three steps

  1. Add the camera

    Open the Photo Studio and press Add Photo Camera. It borrows your game's own camera and hands it back exactly as it was.

  2. Make things photographable

    Select any objects and press Make Selected Photographable. Each gets a name, a journal section and, if you like, behaviours to catch.

  3. Press Play

    C raises the camera, V switches the camera body, Z the lens, X turns it round for a selfie and F is the flash. Space takes the photo. Tab opens the album, J the journal and P the photo mode.

Features

A photo is an event. You decide what it means.

The camera knows what it sees

Recognition
  • What is in the picture: which subjects, how much of each is in frame, how much of the picture it fills.
  • What is in the way: a subject behind a wall counts as hidden, and the photo says so.
  • What it was doing: one line from your own code or an animation event tells a subject it is jumping, eating or asleep.

Two cameras, one component

  • A handheld camera with zoom, steady aim, film and bursts
  • A photo mode: fly or orbit, focus blur with auto focus, filters, frames, guides, a timer, viewpoints and picture shapes
  • The same lens effects in Built-in, URP and HDRP
  • Works with any controller; your camera is only borrowed

Eight cameras to look through

  • SLR viewfinder, camera back, phone, camcorder, instant camera, rangefinder, disposable and a photo booth
  • Players switch between them while the camera is up
  • Each has its own window, picture shape and lens; the saved photo is exactly what its window shows
  • Recolour them, or use your own artwork

Cameras with character

  • Instant prints that develop, a photo booth that prints a strip of four
  • A real flash that lights the saved picture: Auto, On or Off
  • Lenses to swap (wide, standard, telephoto), with locked ones to earn
  • Selfies: one key turns the camera round to face the player
  • Focus lock, a date stamp, each body's own shutter sound, and match a shot

Scores players can read

  • Six criteria, each with its points and a plain reason
  • Medals and rarity tiers
  • Eight presets, from wildlife to landmarks
  • Or no scoring at all

Photo quests

  • Requests from 18 kinds of rule, with AND and OR
  • "A zebra at the pond", "any animal mid-jump"
  • Completed requests raise an event for your game
  • No code needed

Journal and album

  • Discoveries, behaviours seen and best shots
  • Real picture files, up to 2160p, in eight shapes
  • Favourites, delete and save a copy
  • Saved safely, off the main thread

Player screens, ready to use

  • Viewfinder, result, album, journal, requests and options
  • Six ways to show a photo after the shutter, with a switch for every stat
  • Three looks, each fully editable
  • Keyboard, mouse and gamepad, with hints that follow the device
  • Text size, reduced motion and high contrast

Made to be changed

Fits any game
  • Seven starting points: photo safari, photo quests, stealth evidence, camera as a weapon, photo mode only, scrapbook, or fully headless.
  • Every step can be switched off or replaced: capture, recognition, scoring, what is kept, and every screen.
  • The Photo Studio: set up, build requests, see exactly why a view scores what it does, and check the scene for mistakes with one-click fixes.

Included demo scenes

A wildlife trail and a lab to learn in

Meadow Trail has nine animals, six landmarks, six photo requests and a journal to fill. Creator Lab has one station for each idea.

Technical details

Built to fit your project

Unity6000.0.85f1 or newer (tested on 6000.0, 6000.3 and 6000.6)
Render pipelinesBuilt-in, URP and HDRP, with the same lens effects in all three
InputInput Manager, the Input System package (with gamepad), or your own
DependenciesNone. The screens use UI Toolkit, which is part of Unity
PhotosJPG or PNG files in the game's save location, written off the main thread
SourceFull C#, no third-party code, no native plugins
MenuTools > Photoworthy

Good to know

Limits, stated up front

  • Recognition uses colliders to tell what is hidden. Things that should hide a subject need a collider.
  • Photos are local to each player. To share a result in multiplayer, send what you need over your own network code.
  • Photo mode photos do not count for progress by default, so players cannot freeze time for a perfect shot. One switch changes that.
