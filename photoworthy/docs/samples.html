Demo scenes

Two scenes, opened from Tools > Photoworthy > Open Demo. Both work in the Built-in, URP and HDRP pipelines and with either input system, with nothing to convert.

Controls #

Key Gamepad What it does
W A S D, mouse Left stick, right stick Walk and look
C D-pad up Raise and put away the camera
Mouse wheel LB, RB Zoom
Space or left mouse button RT Take the photo
Tab D-pad left Album
J D-pad right Journal and requests
P D-pad down Photo mode
O Start, in a menu Options
L Swap the look of every screen (demo only)
Esc B Back

Gamepad needs the Input System package.

Meadow Trail #

A short wildlife trail: the game the Wildlife Discovery starting point makes.

  • Nine animals of seven kinds. The horse, zebras and cow wander, run and jump; the others stand and hop. Each tells its Photo Subject what it is doing, so photos know.
  • Six landmarks: the Great Oak, the Old Obelisk, the Stone Head, the Stone Ring, the Trail Camp (one subject made of several objects, with its own points) and the Old Canoe.
  • The pond is a Photo Region, used by a request.
  • Six requests, each showing a different kind of rule: any subject, a good portrait facing the camera, any animal mid-jump, two different subjects together, a zebra at the pond, and a silver medal photo of the obelisk.
  • The journal has two sections, Animals and Landmarks, with behaviours to find.
  • Eight camera bodies: the camera comes up as the back of a camera held in front of you. V switches to the SLR viewfinder, phone, camcorder, instant camera, rangefinder, disposable, photo booth and the plain viewfinder. The bodies are assets in Samples/Data/Bodies, there to copy and recolour. Try the instant camera (the print develops) and the photo booth (four photos, one strip).
  • Three lenses: Z swaps between Standard, Wide and Telephoto.
  • Selfies: X turns the camera round. The demo is first person, so the hiker is hidden until then: she is listed under Shown In Selfie on the Photo Camera, and a small script (DemoCharacter) reaches her arm out to the camera.
  • Day and night: N switches to night, where the flash (F) shows what it does.
  • A photo board by the trail head shows your latest photo (a Photo Display), and the photo mode's Viewpoint row jumps to three Photo Spots: Pond Lookout, Under The Oak and Camp Corner.

Things to try: photograph the horse from the front and from behind and compare the result cards; zoom in until "Too small" goes away; wait for a jump; open the photo mode (P) by the pond and try the Lens and Look tabs.

Creator Lab #

A fenced yard with five places to stand. Step on a violet disc and a notice says what to try.

  1. Any object can be a subject. The column was made photographable with one button.
  2. Step sideways for a clear view. A wall hides most of the stone head. The result says "Partly hidden"; one step to the side and it is clear. This is the request "A Clear View".
  3. Catch the jump. The pug jumps often. Catch it in the air for the request "The Right Moment".
  4. Two subjects in one photo. The sheep and the llama, for the request "A Pair".
  5. Press P for photo mode. A pretty corner to fly around.

How the demos are made #

  • Samples/Scripts holds the small scripts the demos use: a first-person walker (DemoWalker), an animal that wanders and reports its behaviour (DemoAnimal, a good example of the one line your own AI needs), surfaces and lights that follow the render pipeline, the look button and the station notices. They are examples, not part of the kit: your game keeps its own controller.
  • Samples/Data holds the subjects, requests, catalogs, profiles and looks. Open them to see how each is set up.
  • Samples/Models holds CC0 models by Quaternius and Kenney. See Third-Party Notices.txt.

The whole Samples folder can be deleted. Nothing else depends on it.

Demo photos #

Photos taken in the demos are saved under the project's persistent data path, in Photos/Meadow Trail Demo and Photos/Creator Lab Demo. Delete those folders to start again, or delete photos in the album.
