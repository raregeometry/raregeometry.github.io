Screens and looks

Everything players see is built in, works with keyboard, mouse and gamepad, and can be restyled, reworded or switched off piece by piece.

The screens #

Screen When What it shows
Viewfinder While a camera is up Corner brackets, a centre mark, optional guide lines, the zoom, film left, the current request, and a mark on each subject the camera would recognise. When photos are a different shape from the screen, the part that will be cut off is dimmed.
Result card After a shot The photo, what it shows, the score and medal, the points for each criterion, the main reasons, new discoveries and completed requests. With Player Chooses, it waits for Keep or Discard.
Notices Any time Short messages such as "New discovery" when the result card is not showing.
Photo mode controls In the photo mode Four tabs of rows: Camera, Lens, Look, Frame.
Album Tab A grid of photos and a detail pane. Favourites, delete, save a copy.
Journal J Subjects by section with best shots and behaviours, and the Requests tab.
Options O The players' own settings.
Key hints On every screen The keys or gamepad buttons for what can be done right now. They follow the device in use.

Switch each one on or off in the profile under Screens. With all of them off the kit draws nothing: listen to the camera's events and build your own UI. See Fit it into your game.

Other switches there: name subjects in the viewfinder only once photographed, guide lines, open the album and journal only while the camera is up, pause the game while a full-screen screen is open, free the mouse cursor while a screen needs it, the shutter flash and the shutter sound.

Looks #

A Photo Look asset holds every colour, corner, line thickness, the font, text size, title capitals and motion time. Three are built in:

  • Field Journal: warm paper, ink text, a rust accent, photos as prints.
  • Modern Viewfinder: dark glass, light text, an amber accent.
  • Instant Print: white cards, round corners, a coral accent, thick print borders.

Make your own with Create > Photoworthy > Look, start from one of the three in its Inspector, change anything, and give it to the profile. A look can be swapped while the game runs:

photoCamera.GetComponent<PhotoScreens>().LookOverride = nightLook;

In the demos, L cycles the three looks.

The kit ships no fonts. Give a look your own font, or it uses Unity's built-in one.

Words #

Every word players read comes from one table, so the screens can be reworded or translated without touching code.

  • Create > Photoworthy > Text Table, add rows (a key and your words), and give it to the profile. Rows you leave out keep the built-in English. Add Every Key in the table's Inspector adds a row for every key with its English words, ready to reword or translate.
  • For a localisation system, set PhotoText.Translate to a function that receives a key and the English words and returns yours.
  • From code: PhotoText.Set("result.newDiscovery", "First sighting!").

Subject names, descriptions, behaviour names and request words are on their own assets.

Players' options #

The options screen lets players set things for themselves. You choose what is offered.

Group Options
Camera Look speed, invert look
Screen Guide lines, subject marks, key hints, shutter flash
Sound Camera sounds volume
Accessibility Text size, reduced motion, high contrast

With nothing set up, every option is offered. To decide yourself, Create > Photoworthy > Player Options:

Setting What it does
Defaults What every option starts at, and what Reset goes back to.
Limits How far the look speed and text size sliders move.
Camera, Screen, Sound, Accessibility Options Which groups the screen offers. An option that is not offered always follows your default.
Remember Keep the player's choices between sessions on this device.

Three starting points: Standard, Simple (sound and accessibility only) and Accessible (wide ranges, larger text). Give the asset to the profile's Player Options. Switch the whole screen off in Screens.

To offer the same options in your own menu:

PhotoPlayerSettings.Current.guideLines = true;
PhotoPlayerSettings.Apply();

Or open the built-in screen from your pause menu: photoCamera.GetComponent<PhotoScreens>().OpenOptions().

Accessibility #

  • Text size scales every word on every screen.
  • Reduced motion makes panels and cards appear at once.
  • High contrast draws solid panels, plain black or white text and thicker lines, whatever the look.
  • Shutter flash can be switched off.
  • Every screen works without a mouse: arrows or D-pad to move, Enter or A to press, Esc or B to back out.
  • Results are never told by colour alone: medals have names, reasons are words.

These also follow the shared accessibility settings that every Rare Geometry product reads, so a settings product (Shipready) or your own code can set them once for the whole game:

RareGeometry.Core.Accessibility.Defaults.ReducedMotion = true;

Sounds #

The shutter, a chime for discoveries, a blip when the camera says no and a tick in menus are made by the kit, with no audio files. Replace any of them with your own clip, and set the volume, in the profile under Sounds.

Your own panel settings #

The Photo Screens component (added to the camera automatically) has a Sorting Order, so the screens draw above or below your other UI Toolkit panels, and a slot for your own Panel Settings.
