Docs/Photoworthy/Features

Cameras

One Photo Camera component gives players two ways to take photos. Use either, both or neither (your own code can take photos with no camera screen at all).

The Photo Camera component #

Setting What it does
Game Camera The camera your game renders with. Empty finds the one tagged MainCamera.
Profile The Photo Profile: how photography works in your game. Empty uses a built-in profile with everything on.
Handheld Viewpoint Where the handheld camera looks from, if not the game camera's own place (a third-person game puts it at the character's eyes).
Pause While Open Components switched off while any camera screen is open (your shooting script).
Pause In Photo Mode Components switched off while the photo mode is open (your player controller).
Events Opened, Closed, Photo Taken, Photo Kept, Photo Discarded, Photo Saved, Shot Refused.

Your game camera is borrowed, never replaced. While a screen is open the kit changes its field of view and, in the photo mode, its position. On closing, position, rotation, field of view and any lens effect are put back exactly.

The handheld camera #

The in-world camera, used while the game runs: raise it, zoom, take the photo. Settings are in the profile under Handheld.

Setting What it does
Enabled Players can raise it.
Widest View, Closest Zoom The zoom range, as fields of view.
Zoom Speed, Zoom Smoothing How zooming feels.
Controls Look Let the kit turn the view while the camera is up. Leave off when your own controller keeps looking around.
Look Speed, Steady Aim, Slow Look When Zoomed How aiming feels, with a hold-to-steady button for long lenses.
World What the game does while it is up: Keep Running, Pause or Slow Motion.
Counts For Progress Photos from it can be judged, complete requests and fill the journal.

The photo mode #

A free camera for beautiful pictures: fly, zoom, tilt, set the lens, hide the controls, take the photo. Settings are in the profile under Photo Mode.

Setting What it does
Enabled Players can open it.
World Pause (the default), Slow Motion or Keep Running.
Move Speed, Fast Multiplier, Look Speed, Smoothing How flying feels.
Travel Radius How far the camera can fly from where it started. 0 is no limit.
Collide, Collision Radius, Collision Layers Stop the camera at walls and floors.
Widest View, Closest Zoom, Max Tilt The lens and tilt limits.
Counts For Progress Off by default, so players cannot freeze time and fly to a perfect shot. Switch on if your game wants it.

The controls panel has four tabs: Camera (zoom, tilt, guide lines), Lens (focus blur, focus distance, blur amount, brightness, contrast, colour), Look (filter, warmth, tint, dark corners) and Frame. Up and down pick a row, left and right change it; the mouse works too. H hides the controls for a clean view, R resets the lens.

Lens effects in every pipeline #

Focus blur, brightness, contrast, colour, warmth, tint and dark corners work the same in all three render pipelines, with nothing to set up:

  • Built-in: the kit's own image effect. No Post Processing package needed.
  • URP and HDRP: the kit drives a temporary Volume while a camera screen is open, and removes it on closing. Post processing must be on for the camera, which is the default in new projects.

The saved picture has the same look as the screen.

Filters and frames #

Seven filters (Warm, Cool, Vivid, Faded, Black and White, Sepia, Noir) and four frames (White border, Instant print, Black border, Film bars) are built in. Make your own with Create > Photoworthy > Filter and Frame, and list them in the profile's Filters and Frames. A frame with the Picture style uses your own artwork. Frames are drawn into the saved picture.

Film and pace #

Under Film in the profile:

Setting What it does
Limited Photos use up film. Off means players can shoot for ever.
Starting Shots, Max Shots How much film the camera starts with and can hold. Give more with AddFilm.
Cooldown Seconds before the next photo.
Refund Rejected Photos that are not kept give their shot back.
Photo Mode Uses Film The photo mode uses film too.

Which photos are kept #

Under Review in the profile, Keep decides: Everything, Only With A Subject, Only Good Shots, or Player Chooses (the result card waits for Keep or Discard). For your own rule, set the camera's DecideKeep in code. See Scripting.

When the camera says no #

Block the camera from your game (a cutscene, a no-photo zone) with Blocked = true, or give it a rule with CanOpen and CanShoot. When a photo is refused, the Shot Refused event says why: no film, album full, not ready, or blocked.

Keys #

All keys are in the profile under Keys, with gamepad buttons added when the Input System is installed. Switch Built In Input off to drive everything from your own input: call OpenHandheld, OpenPhotoMode, TakePhoto and Close. See Fit it into your game.
