Docs/Photoworthy/Features

Scoring

Judging photos is optional. When it is on, every point has a reason a player can read.

Scoring modes #

A Scoring Profile asset (Create > Photoworthy) has a mode:

Mode What a photo gets
Off Nothing. Photos are never judged.
Identify Only What it shows. No points, no medals.
Pass Or Fail Good enough or not, against a pass mark.
Points A score from 0 to the top score, with a medal.
Custom Your own scorer asset decides.

The six criteria #

Criterion Question it answers Default weight
Framing Is the subject where it should be in the picture (centre, or on a thirds line)? 25
Size Does it fill the right share of the picture? 20
Visibility How much of it can be seen, not hidden or cut off? 20
Facing Does it face the camera? 15
Action How rare is what it is doing? 15
Context What else is in the picture: other subjects, special places. 5

Each criterion can be switched off; the others then share the points, so the top score stays the same. A subject with no front is never judged on Facing.

The main subject #

A photo can show several subjects, and each gets its own score sheet. The photo's own score is its main subject's: the one under the centre mark (the nearest, when several are), or otherwise the largest one near the middle. So a far-off landmark never takes a photo away from the animal the player aimed at. Requests about a particular subject always use that subject's own score.

Medals and rarity #

With points, a photo earns Bronze for any counted subject, Silver from 50% of the top score, Gold from 70% and Diamond from 90%. The marks are settings.

Quality and rarity are kept apart on purpose. The medal says how good the photo is. The tier (1 to 4) says how rare the moment was. A plain photo of a rare behaviour and a perfect photo of a common one are both worth showing in the journal, in different ways.

Reasons #

Every result carries plain reasons: what held it back ("Too small", "Facing away", "Partly hidden", "Cut off", "Off centre") and what went well ("Well framed", "Clear view", "Rare moment"). The result card shows the most important ones. Switch them off with Show Hints in the profile's Review settings. All the words can be changed; see Screens and looks.

Presets #

Pick one in the Scoring Profile's Inspector, then adjust.

Preset For
Wildlife The six criteria in balance: animals and creatures.
Portrait People and characters: facing and thirds framing matter most.
Landmark Buildings and views: framing, size and a clear view. No front, no action.
Small Object Small things: getting close and a clear view.
Group Shot Several subjects together: company counts.
Action Moment The right moment: what the subject is doing matters most.
Identify Only No points.
Pass Or Fail Good enough or not.

A subject asset can carry its own scoring asset (Scoring Override), so a landmark and an animal in the same game are each judged fairly.

The Score Inspector #

Tools > Photoworthy > Score Inspector answers "why did this photo get that score?" without guessing.

  • Judge Game Camera judges what the Photo Camera's game camera sees right now. Judge Scene View judges what the Scene view sees: frame a subject in the Scene view and press it.
  • For each subject it lists every criterion with its points and reason, the numbers behind them (share of the picture, share visible, facing, distance) and the points checked.
  • Nearby but not counted lists subjects that were close to counting, with the reason each did not.

It works in Edit Mode, so you can tune a scoring asset and watch the same view change.

Your own scorer #

For rules no setting covers, make an asset class and set the Scoring Profile's mode to Custom:

using System.Collections.Generic;
using RareGeometry.Photoworthy;
using UnityEngine;

[CreateAssetMenu(menuName = "My Game/Night Scorer")]
public class NightScorer : CustomPhotoScorer
{
    public override void Evaluate(PhotoContext context, IReadOnlyList<SubjectSighting> sightings,
        ScoringProfile profile, PhotoEvaluation result)
    {
        // One score per subject in the picture; Finish picks the best as the photo's score and hands out the medal.
        foreach (var sighting in sightings)
        {
            var night = context.GameTime % 600f > 300f;
            result.Subjects.Add(new SubjectScore { Sighting = sighting, Score = night ? 90f : 40f });
        }
        result.Finish(profile);
    }
}

To keep the built-in judging and only adjust the result, listen to PhotoEvents.PhotoEvaluated and change the photo there. See Scripting.
